# DOCUMENT 0.5 — THE MEMBRANE SPECIFICATION
### The Skin of the Body — What May Cross the Boundary, in Both Directions

**System:** Joint (the body's boundary organ) · **Tier:** 0 / 7 (Constitutional boundary; operationally part of the interface tier) · **Licenses:** `LicenseRef-MVL-2.0` and `LicenseRef-AVL-2.0` at their respective edges · **Maturity:** InDevelopment (the doctrine is specifiable now; several enforcement components depend on Research-tier pieces — stated below) · **Authority:** Subordinate to 0.1 (the Invariant Specification) and 0.4 (Coercion-Resistance); supreme over the tunnel, filter, and bridge implementations it unifies. · **© Maher**

## Preamble — why the body needs a skin

Every organ specified so far is internal: the spine that binds the builders, the heart that circulates issuance, the hands that settle, the mind that gates, the conscience that vetoes, the immune system that reverses fraud. But a body that holds planetary value and touches the outside world needs a **boundary** — a single organ that governs what crosses it, inward and outward. Until now this question has been answered in three scattered places: Aevum's ethical filter at the experience edge, the Compliance/tunnel-licensing layer, and the ISO 20022 / crypto-bridge ingress. Those answers are correct individually but fragmented, and "what may cross the boundary of a body that holds planetary value?" is a single coherent question that deserves a single coherent answer. This document is that answer. It unifies the three into one organ — the **membrane** — and gives it one defining law.

## The defining property (the inversion, applied to the boundary)

The membrane obeys the same anatomical inversion as every other part of the body: **it refuses, but it cannot reach.** Concretely, the membrane has exactly two powers, both negative:

It can **refuse what tries to enter** (an asset, a value flow, an attestation, a settlement message that fails the boundary tests), and it can **refuse to let the body reach toward harm** (block an outbound flow or address that the user's own rules, or the constitutional global-block process, forbid). 

It has **no power to pull anything in or push anything out on its own.** The membrane never originates a transfer, never mints, never debits, never initiates a settlement. It is a **selective barrier, not a mouth.** This matters because a boundary organ with the power to *initiate* would be a place where the body could be made to act against a person from its own edge — exactly the capability the inversion exists to deny. By construction, the membrane can only say "no"; it can never say "do." A value flow happens only when a *holder* authorizes it (I10) and the *ledger* posts it (I1–I3); the membrane's role is solely to stand at the door and refuse what must not pass.

This single property resolves a tension that scattered the boundary across three documents: the filter, the tunnels, and the bridges all do the *same kind of thing* — they are gates, not engines — and unifying them under "the skin refuses but never reaches" makes that shared nature explicit and auditable.

## The three faces of one organ

The membrane has three faces, but one nature. Each face is a place where the body meets a different part of the outside world.

**Face 1 — the experience edge (Aevum's ethical filter).** This is where users and their wallets meet the body. The filter is **user-sovereign by default**: each wallet sets its own rules about what assets it will hold, surface, or route, and the protocol provides multi-sourced, contestable labels rather than imposing a single verdict. The filter can refuse to *surface or route* a transfer at the experience edge, but — per AVL-2.0 §A-§C.14 and I10 — it can never itself author a Meridian balance change; it proposes through the signed event contract for the system of record to validate. A *global* block (one imposed on all users) is reserved for legally-compelled cases and requires the full constitutional process: timelock, public written justification, and an appeal path (AVL-2.0 §A-§C.10). Silent or unilateral global blocking is a breach, because a system that can blacklist a national currency wields a foreign-policy power, and *Adl* requires that power be visible, costly, and contestable.

**Face 2 — the regulated tunnels (Compliance / licensing).** This is where the body meets fiat and reserve-backed value through licensed institutions. The governing doctrine, stated firmly: **tunnels are licensed entities, not protocol features.** A fiat on/off-ramp or CBDC connector is operated by a party that holds the relevant license in its jurisdiction and bears the legal obligations that come with custody of fiat. At this face the membrane applies the inbound controls the law requires — sanctions and AML screening, travel-rule data handling, and real-time reserve attestation (the proof, per MVL-2.0 §M-§C.15 and artifact 8.3, that reserve-backed value is actually backed). The membrane *refuses* a flow that fails these checks; it does not *originate* the underlying settlement, which remains a consensual act by the holder (I10) posted by the ledger (I1).

**Face 3 — the crypto bridges (ingress/egress).** This is where the body meets external chains. Bridged assets are *represented*, never trusted as the source of truth: an external asset is locked on its native chain and a representation is minted within the body only after the bridge adapter confirms the lock to sufficient depth, and the authoritative balance always lives in the body's own double-entry ledger (I1–I3). The membrane applies the **ethical filter at ingress** — an asset that fails the user's rules or a constitutional global block is refused entry before any representation is minted — and applies **send-strict discipline at egress** (see ISO 20022 below for the structured-address rule, which the membrane enforces on outbound messages to traditional rails).

## Inbound controls (what the membrane refuses to admit)

On the way in, the membrane refuses: assets and addresses on applicable sanctions registries (at regulated tunnels where law requires screening); flows that fail AML checks where the operating tunnel is legally obligated to perform them; bridge deposits not confirmed to the required depth on the source chain; ISO 20022 messages it cannot validate; and any asset that the receiving user's own ethical-filter rules reject, or that a constitutional global block forbids. The membrane's refusal is *informative*: in the user-sovereign default, an asset the user's rules disfavor is *flagged with a warning* and the user decides; only at the regulated-tunnel face (legal screening) and the constitutional-global-block level (compelled) is refusal mandatory and non-overridable by the individual user. This preserves user sovereignty (the user chooses their own ethics) while honoring the two cases where individual choice cannot govern: the law, and a properly-enacted constitutional block.

## Outbound controls (what the membrane refuses to let the body reach toward)

On the way out, the membrane enforces two things. First, the **user's own egress rules and any constitutional global block** — the body will not reach toward an asset or counterparty the user has chosen to avoid, or that the constitutional process has blocked. Second, **send-strict structured addressing** for traditional-rail settlement (see ISO 20022 below). Crucially, the outbound membrane *refuses*; it does not *send*. The actual outbound value movement is, like every value movement, a consensual holder-authorized act (I10) posted by the ledger (I1) — the membrane only stands at the door and refuses egress that violates a rule. There is no membrane power to push value out of a person's account on the membrane's own initiative.

## ISO 20022 posture — receive-tolerant, send-strict (born post-coexistence)

The membrane is **born after** the cross-border coexistence window (which closed 22 November 2025), so it is **structured-address-native from line one**. Two rules govern its traditional-rail face:

**Receive-tolerant (accept hybrid, normalize).** Counterparties — banks and corridors — were themselves still hybrid through 2026. The membrane therefore *accepts* inbound hybrid (partially structured) party addresses and **normalizes** them to fully structured form internally, so that legitimate inbound settlements from compliant-but-still-hybrid counterparties are not bounced.

**Send-strict (emit structured-only).** The membrane *emits* only fully structured party addresses outbound, in line with the November 2026 SWIFT rule rejecting unstructured postal addresses. A reserve adapter that cannot produce a fully structured address does not get to send; the membrane refuses the egress rather than emit a message that will bounce or that fails the standard.

This receive-tolerant / send-strict asymmetry is the operationally correct posture for the 2026 transition, and it is itself an instance of the membrane's nature: tolerant at the door coming in (refuse only what is genuinely non-compliant), strict at the door going out (refuse anything that would carry the body's value into the world in a non-conforming form).

## The GAS ingress boundary (where the skin meets the identity provider)

The membrane is also where the body meets its external identity and personhood provider (GAS, and any other provider of N). The constitutional rule (I9, and MVL-2.0 §M-§C.11 / AVL-2.0 §A-§C.11) governs this face absolutely: **imported risk signals stop at the skin.** GAS's cross-relying-party reputation graph and behavioral signals may cross the membrane *only* to serve authentication-time fraud prevention, and may **never** cross further into any value-affecting, minting, personhood-aggregation, or rights-affecting decision. The membrane enforces this as a one-way, typed boundary: an authentication-fraud signal is admitted into the auth layer and is structurally prevented (by the compile-time wall verified in `PersonhoodBoundaryTest`) from reaching the value layer. This is the membrane's most important inbound refusal — it refuses to let a cross-context behavioral score become the basis of any decision about a person's money, which is precisely the social-credit-by-import path the Red-Team Dossier (0.3) flags.

## What the membrane is forbidden to do (the structural denials)

To make the inversion auditable, the membrane is forbidden, by construction, from: originating any transfer; minting or burning any value; authoring any entry against any account (it has no ledger-write capability — it can only *propose refusal*, and the ledger does the posting); debiting a personal balance under any circumstance (I6/I7/I10 hold at the boundary as everywhere); admitting raw biometric/health/neural data (I8 — the membrane refuses such payloads at ingress); or letting a provider's cross-context score reach a value decision (I9). The membrane's database role has no INSERT/UPDATE privilege on `entries` or `transactions`; it writes only to its own label, screening-result, and refusal-log tables. A property test asserts the membrane has no path to a balance change.

## Governance of the membrane's rules

User-level filter rules are set by users and need no governance. Label sources are multi-sourced and contestable (a user or asset issuer may challenge a label, and labels carry provenance). The one membrane power that *is* governed at the constitutional level is the **global block** — because it is the one membrane action that overrides individual user sovereignty. A global block requires the constitutional process (high quorum, timelock, public written justification, appeal path) and is logged to the transparency record; it may never be invoked silently or unilaterally. The Humility Clause (ML-2.0 §G.5, mirrored in both child licenses) governs here too: a global block enacted in bad faith, to suppress legitimate dissent, or to entrench the Steward's advantage is void.

## Honest caveats

Three. First, the membrane's regulated-tunnel face depends on real-world licensing that is jurisdiction-specific and 2026-fluid; the doctrine ("tunnels are licensed entities") is firm but the jurisdiction map is loose and lives in the Regulatory Framework (3.3), not here. Second, the user-sovereign default places real responsibility on users to set their own ethical rules; the system must ship sensible, clearly-explained defaults and contestable labels so sovereignty does not become a burden that pushes users toward harmful assets through inattention — this is a usability-meets-ethics problem that the User Docs (10.3) must solve, and it is currently InDevelopment. Third, the anti-correlation GAS wall is only as strong as the typed boundary and its test; until `PersonhoodBoundaryTest` is implemented and the compile-time boundary proven, the GAS-ingress refusal is partly procedural and is tracked in the Coercion-Resistance companion register (0.4) as a hardening item.
