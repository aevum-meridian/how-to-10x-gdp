# DOCUMENT 3.4 — THE MATURITY & ABANDONMENT LEDGER
### Honest Labels, Exit Criteria, and the Courage to Stop

**System:** Joint · **Tier:** 3 · **Maturity:** this document is itself Shipped-grade (it is the source of truth for all other labels) · **Surfaced at:** `/api/v1/maturity` · **© Maher**

## 0. Why this document has unusual authority

In a system built on *Sidq*, the maturity ledger is load-bearing: it is the single source of truth that prevents every other surface — marketing, documentation, governance, user-facing copy — from overclaiming. The licenses bind to it (AVL-2.0 §A-§C.13: no surface may present a Research capability as Shipped). Changing a label is a governance act, logged. And uniquely among maturity systems, this ledger carries **both exit criteria** (proceed when X) **and abandonment criteria** (stop and redesign if Y) — because a system intended for a third of humanity needs not only a definition of "ready" but the courage to define "stop."

## 1. The four labels

**Shipped** — built, audited where required, in production, and honestly available. **InDevelopment** — actively being built, not yet production-ready, must not be presented as available. **Research** — an open problem, possibly unsolved at scale, shipped (if at all) only as honestly-labelled partial coverage. **DeprecatedRemoved** — retired, kept in the record for historical honesty (e.g., the EEG-attention experiment $FOCUS, retired on the no-neural-data principle).

## 3. The most important row

The last row deserves emphasis because it is the one most likely to be tempting to overclaim. The 8-billion-users figure is **Research**, and its abandonment criterion is explicit and inverted from how ambition usually works: *if the system cannot responsibly serve the stated population, the population claim is revised downward — the safety floors are never relaxed to reach the number.* The scale serves the people; the people are never sacrificed to the scale. This row is the maturity ledger's expression of the whole project's thesis applied to its own ambition.

## 4. Governance of labels

A label change is a parametric governance act (Tier P), logged to the transparency record, with the simulation/audit evidence that justifies it attached. A subsystem may be *promoted* (Research → InDevelopment → Shipped) only when its exit criterion is met and the evidence is published; it must be *demoted or deprecated* when its abandonment criterion is met, and demotion is not a failure to hide but an act of *Sidq* to record. The Deprecated entries are never deleted — the honest history of what the project tried and chose to stop is part of its trustworthiness.

## 5. Honest caveat

A maturity ledger is only honest if it is actually consulted and enforced — the binding to the licenses and the public endpoint are what give it teeth, but the deeper safeguard is cultural: the project must treat demoting a subsystem to Research, or deprecating a currency, as a *virtue* rather than an embarrassment, because the alternative — quietly shipping Research-grade capability as if it were ready — is the exact dishonesty the whole instrument exists to forbid.
